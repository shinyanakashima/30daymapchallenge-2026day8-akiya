If people moved into Japan’s akiya, what year would each place go back to?

空き家に移住したら、街はいつに戻れるか？

#30DayMapChallenge 2026 · Day 8: Utopia — “A map of something which could be.”

What the map shows

Japan has about 3.86 million vacant dwellings that are not held for rent, for sale or as second homes (2023). If people moved into all of them at each prefecture’s average household size, 8.67 million people would move in. For each prefecture, the map shows the census year whose population it would return to, in three cases of where the people come from.

Assumptions

  • Akiya here are vacant dwellings with no specific use (賃貸・売却用及び二次的住宅を除く空き家), 2023 Housing and Land Survey. Dwellings held for rent or sale and second homes such as holiday houses are not included.
  • Many of these houses could not be lived in as they are. This is a calculation of what could be, not a forecast.
  • People per house: average members per private household (一般世帯の1世帯当たり人員) of each prefecture, 2020 census.
  • New population: 2025 census preliminary count plus the people moving in. In B, everyone comes from Tokyo (moves into Tokyo’s own akiya are not counted). In C, everyone comes from Tokyo, Kanagawa, Saitama, Chiba, Aichi and Osaka, split by their population.
  • Return year: the latest census year (every five years, 1920–2020; 1945 not used) in which the prefecture had at least the new population. For prefectures that lose people, the latest census year with no more than the new population. No interpolation.
  • In B and C the population of Japan as a whole does not change.
  • Okinawa is shown in an inset box, not in its true position. The Ogasawara Islands (Tokyo) are not shown.
